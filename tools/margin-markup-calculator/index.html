Understanding Margin vs. Markup

One of the most common mistakes in retail and business is confusing profit margin with markup. While both metrics relate to profit, they look at it from different perspectives. Understanding the difference is crucial for accurately pricing your products and forecasting revenue.

How the Calculation Works

Here are the fundamental formulas used in this calculator:

  • Gross Profit: The absolute monetary amount you make on a sale.
    Formula: Selling Price - Cost Price
  • Profit Margin: The percentage of the selling price that is profit. It answers the question: "For every dollar of revenue, how much is profit?"
    Formula: (Gross Profit / Selling Price) × 100
  • Markup: The percentage of the cost price that is added to determine the selling price. It answers the question: "How much did we add to the cost?"
    Formula: (Gross Profit / Cost Price) × 100

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Why is Margin always lower than Markup?

Because margin is divided by the selling price (a larger number), while markup is divided by the cost price (a smaller number). A 100% markup (doubling your cost) only equates to a 50% margin. It is mathematically impossible to have a margin of 100% unless your cost is $0.

Which metric should I use for my business?

Most financial statements, investors, and accounting software focus on Profit Margin. It gives a clearer picture of profitability relative to total sales. However, Markup is often used by buyers and pricing strategists when determining the retail price from a wholesale cost.

Can my markup be over 100%?

Absolutely! If you buy an item for $10 and sell it for $30, your profit is $20. Your markup is 200% ($20 / $10), while your margin is 66.6% ($20 / $30). High markups are common in industries like jewelry, cosmetics, and beverages.
